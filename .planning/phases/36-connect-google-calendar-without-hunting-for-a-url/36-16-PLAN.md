<objective>
**WINDOWS entry 14:** on 2026-11-02 the owner unticked Home and ticked spunkl, and the device's Hive held
Family (`3935F311-…`), which the screen then showed ticked. It was logged as UNPROVEN, most likely a tap
during the asynchronous list load caused by entry 12.

### What the investigation found — reported plainly

**A plain, stationary tap cannot store the wrong id.** `_toggleCalendar` keys by `calendar.id`; each
`CheckboxListTile`'s `onChanged` closes over the calendar it was built for; and
`SettingsNotifier.setSelectedCalendarIds` replaces the in-memory list synchronously before it awaits Hive,
so two quick taps cannot read a stale selection. No mechanism was found in the toggle path itself.

**Mechanism A — real, and the likeliest cause of the observed tick.** Until plan 36-14, the device list was
torn down and re-fetched on every "Allow calendar access" tap — which, because of entry 12, was every
re-open — and on every mobile "Sync now". The CTA card collapsed to a spinner and then the list appeared in
its place, moving every row under a finger aimed at the previous layout. The re-fetched list also comes back
in whatever order EventKit gives: `device_calendar_plus_ios-0.7.0`'s `CalendarService.swift` iterates
`eventStore.calendars(for: .event)` and appends in that order with no sort, Apple documents no order for that
call, and nothing between the plugin and `_calendarRows` sorts. Plan 36-14 removed the teardown (restore on
open; "Sync now" in place). **This plan makes the order deterministic**, so even a re-fetch on the next open
puts every row where it was.

**Mechanism B — real, latent on 2026-11-02, armed for the next sitting.** The Google section renders ABOVE
the device section. With Google connected, its list loads over the network, while the device list (after
36-14) restores almost instantly from the local store. So the device rows appear first and then jump down
by the whole height of the Google list at an arbitrary moment. On 2026-11-02 Google was **not** connected —
the screenshot shows the Connect card, and every block from the spunkl-only sync hashed to spunkl — so B
cannot have caused the observed tick. But the next device sitting connects Google (R2's R4 and Section B
items), which would arm it and could produce a false "entry 14 reproduces". **This plan moves the device
section above the Google section** — locally available content above network-loaded content.

**Not mechanisms, and not changed:** keys on `CheckboxListTile` matter only if the list is re-ordered
between one pointer-down and its pointer-up; with no in-place teardown and a deterministic order, the rows
are built from one list per open and cannot re-order mid-gesture, so a key has nothing to protect — adding
one would be an untestable change. The account-groups map is a Dart `{}` literal (a `LinkedHashMap`,
insertion-ordered) and is deterministic once its input list is.

**Residual, recorded rather than fixed:** the skipped-events banner sits at the top (the owner's own ask,
entry 10). When a sync the user just triggered turns the skipped set from empty to non-empty, the banner
appears and shifts the list down once. It follows his own tap and is not a load-in race; moving the banner
would undo entry 10's fix.

**Closure:** the code half is this plan. Whether a tick ever lands wrong again is the owner's re-test in
`36-UAT-R3.md` (plan 36-17). If it reproduces with both mechanisms removed, it is a new defect with a new
cause, and the device Hive read is the next step.

### Decided here
The section order change is **reversible** (one line) and visible. It contradicts nothing locked — 36-06's
"Google section first" was an implementation choice, not a ruling (D-36-03 rules only that both sources
coexist). The UAT document must tell the owner the order changed and why, so he can ask for it back
knowing the cost.

Purpose: a tick lands on the calendar the user aimed at.
Output: a deterministic device order and a section order that cannot shift loaded rows.
</objective>
