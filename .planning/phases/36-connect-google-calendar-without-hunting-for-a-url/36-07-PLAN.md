**Section B — Phase 36's items.** One numbered item each, each with a stated expectation so he knows
what he is judging:

1. **The button.** Settings, Calendars. Is there a "Connect Google Calendar" button? Tap it. Does

2. **The consent screen's own words.** Before approving, read what Google says Canopy is asking for.

3. **Cancel first, on purpose.** Back out of the sheet without approving. Does the app return cleanly

   secret, which is the premise D-36-01 rests on.
5. **The expiry, forced rather than waited for.** Go to `myaccount.google.com/permissions`, find
   Canopy, and remove its access. Come back to the app and sync (Sync now, or a re-check-in). **Does
   it say the sign-in expired, in plain words, with a button to reconnect?** Does the Settings row say
   it too? Then tap Reconnect and confirm it actually works. This is CALAUTH-03, and revoking is the
   same wire failure as the 7-day expiry he already accepted — it just does not take a week.
   Also: while you are offline (turn wifi off), sync again. Does it say something about the sync
   failing rather than telling you your login expired? Those are supposed to be two different
   sentences.
6. **The double-tick — the one that needs your judgement, not a test.** Tick the SAME calendar under
   Google and under This device, deliberately. **Does the warning appear, and does it read clearly
   enough that it would stop you doing this by accident?** Then ⟳ Re-check-in and look at a day with
   a meeting on it: does the meeting genuinely appear twice? It should — nothing de-duplicates it,
   because you ruled that both sources coexist and Canopy must not quietly undo a configuration you
   chose. Untick one side and re-check-in to confirm it goes back to once.
7. **Everything still works without any of it.** Disconnect Google, deny or ignore the device
   permission, and confirm the app is fully usable: add a commitment by hand, run a check-in, get a
   day. That is CAL-04, and it is the guarantee that makes all of the above optional rather than
   load-bearing.
8. **Canopy changed nothing.** Open Google Calendar. Are the events exactly as you left them? That is
   CAL-03, now additionally enforced by the read-only scope rather than only by our code.

  <context>
Four things rest entirely on this session:

**Assumption A6** — that `googleapis_auth`'s refresh grant succeeds against an iOS-registered client
with no secret. Google's own native-app documentation says refresh tokens are always returned for
installed applications and that the secret parameter is not applicable to iOS clients, which is the
premise D-36-01 was ruled on. It has not been exercised against a real client. If it is wrong, the
refresh path moves to the other library and CALAUTH-03's detection has to be re-derived against a
different error shape — a real change, not a tweak.

**Assumption A7** — that every iOS cancellation path surfaces as the one exception the code catches.
The catch was deliberately left narrow rather than widened speculatively, because a broad catch would
swallow real failures. Item 3 is what tells us whether narrow was right.

**Pitfall 4** — the bundle identifier. It changed mid-phase under D-36-02 from the `com.example`
placeholder to `com.danjjohnson.canopy`, and the Cloud Console iOS client binds to it. A mismatch
shows up as an unhelpful client error or a consent sheet that never completes, and reads exactly like
a code bug. `tools/build-ios.sh` prints the identifier the build will use — compare it to what Cloud
Console shows before concluding anything is broken in the code.

**D-36-03's mitigation.** You ruled that both sources coexist and you tick per calendar, against the
recommendation, with the failure mode on screen in the option's own preview. The condition that keeps
that ruling honest is that the double-tick is visible at the moment of ticking rather than after a
check-in. Item 6 is where you say whether it actually is.
  </context>
