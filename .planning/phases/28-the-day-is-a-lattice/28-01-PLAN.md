## The cell rule — derive every expected sequence from this, do not guess

A **cell** is 30 minutes. `N` comes from `_moodBreakCadence` `{1:2, 2:3, 3:4, 4:4, 5:5}`, keyed on
the check-in mood. Counting discretionary work chunks from 1:

| Chunk k | Emits | Next cell starts |
|---------|-------|------------------|
| `k % N != 0` (ordinary) | work@S (25 min), shortBreak@S+25 (5 min) | S+30 |
| `k % N == 0` (boundary) | work@S (25 min), shortBreak@S+25 (5 min), longBreak@S+30 (30 min) | S+60 |

- The first cell of a free slot starts at that slot's lattice-aligned start; with no commitments and
  no `startFloorMinutes` that is **480** (8:00 AM).
- A boundary cycle is therefore `N × 30 + 30` minutes — at N=3, 120 minutes, back on a clean
  two-hour boundary. That is the phase goal, stated as arithmetic.
- STEP E trims a **trailing shortBreak**; a **trailing longBreak survives** (D-05).

### What LATTICE-01 actually asserts — read this before writing the mod-30 loop

`28-RESEARCH.md` and `28-PATTERNS.md` both sketch the invariant as "every chunk's start `% 30 == 0`".
**That sketch is wrong and will fail on a correct engine.** A short break starts 25 minutes into its
cell — it is the tail of the cell, not a new one. The correct, testable invariant is per chunk type:

| Chunk type | start | duration |
|------------|-------|----------|
| discretionary work | `% 30 == 0` | 25 |
| shortBreak | `% 30 == 25` | 5 |
| longBreak | `% 30 == 0` | 30 |
| commitment-anchored work (`anchoredStartMinutes != null`) | **exempt — never rounded (D-01)** | as entered |

Equivalently: every *cell* starts on a 30-minute boundary. Write the assertion that way.

### The owner's day — expected result, verbatim

mood 3 (N=4), `blocks: []`, 4 habits → exactly 4 discretionary work chunks:

`W@480(25) SB@505(5) W@510(25) SB@535(5) W@540(25) SB@565(5) W@570(25) SB@595(5) LB@600(30)`

`result.length == 9`, `result.last.chunkType == ChunkType.longBreak`, `result.last.durationMinutes == 30`.
Today this returns 7 chunks ending in work, with **no long break anywhere** — that is the reported bug.
